How are memories made?

4 min read · AXON

From a new fact to something you still know next year — and the patient who taught science where memory lives.

The patient H.M.

In 1953, a young man known as H.M. had parts of both temporal lobes, including the hippocampus, removed to stop severe epilepsy. The seizures improved, but he could no longer form new long-term memories of facts and events. He could still learn new movement skills, though he did not remember practising them. His case showed that memory is not one thing and that the hippocampus is key for new memories.

Three steps

Encoding is taking information in — paying attention matters. Consolidation is making it stable: over hours, days and especially during sleep, memories are strengthened and gradually rely less on the hippocampus and more on the cortex. Retrieval is bringing a memory back — and every recall slightly rebuilds it.

What changes in the brain

At the level of cells, learning strengthens some synapses and weakens others. Repeated use of a connection makes the receiving neuron respond more strongly — one of the main ways memories are thought to be stored.

Memory research in Baku

Scientists in Azerbaijan study memory too: one Baku research line has spent decades on brain proteins that seem to act as a brake on storing memories. You can find these papers on AXON’s Articles page under “From Azerbaijan”.

Study tips that follow

Test yourself instead of rereading, spread practice over several days, and sleep well before and after learning. All three work with how the brain stores memories, not against it.
