Neuron cell

The neuron is the nervous system’s signalling unit: a cell body (soma) often on the order of tens of micrometres across in cortex, branching dendrites that collect thousands of synaptic inputs, a single axon that can range from millimetres to more than a metre in humans, and axon terminals that pass the signal to the next cell. Explore here to see how these parts are arranged in space and why that geometry shapes excitability, conduction, and plasticity.

Chemical synapse

A chemical synapse is the communication gap between neurons: the presynaptic side packages neurotransmitter in vesicles and releases it at the active zone; molecules cross a synaptic cleft only about tens of nanometres wide; the postsynaptic membrane holds receptors and scaffolding (including the postsynaptic density) that convert binding into electrical and biochemical changes in the receiving cell. This scene highlights why that short distance and precise alignment make transmission fast and reliable.
