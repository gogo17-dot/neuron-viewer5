What is epilepsy?

4 min read · AXON

One of the most common brain conditions — what a seizure is, how it is treated and how to help someone having one.

A storm of electricity

Normally neurons fire in an orderly way. During a seizure, a group of neurons suddenly fires too much and all together. What happens depends on where it starts: it may cause jerking of an arm, a strange feeling or a few seconds of staring — or, if it spreads across the whole brain, loss of consciousness with stiffening and jerking of the body.

Epilepsy is a tendency

One seizure does not mean epilepsy. Epilepsy means the brain has a lasting tendency to have seizures without an obvious trigger. Around 50 million people worldwide live with it. It is not contagious and it is not a mental illness.

Diagnosis and treatment

Doctors use the patient’s story, an EEG (which records the brain’s electrical activity) and brain scans. Most people can become seizure-free with the right medicine — the World Health Organization estimates up to 70%. For some, surgery or other treatments help.

First aid for a seizure

Stay calm and stay with the person. Note the time. Move hard or sharp objects away and put something soft under their head. Do not hold them down and never put anything in their mouth. When the jerking stops, turn them gently onto their side. Call 103 if the seizure lasts more than 5 minutes, if another follows, if the person is injured, pregnant or has trouble breathing, or if it is their first seizure.
