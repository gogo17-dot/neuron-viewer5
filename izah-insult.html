Stroke: know the signs

3 min read · AXON

A stroke is a brain emergency. Knowing the signs and calling 103 fast can save a life.

What happens

A stroke happens when blood stops reaching part of the brain. Most strokes are caused by a blocked artery (ischaemic stroke); the rest by a burst blood vessel (haemorrhagic stroke). Without blood, brain cells start dying within minutes — in a large untreated stroke, close to two million neurons are lost every minute. Doctors say: “time is brain”.

Remember F.A.S.T.

Face: one side of the face droops — ask the person to smile. Arms: one arm is weak or drifts down when both are raised. Speech: words are slurred, strange or missing. Time: if you see any of these, call 103 at once and note the time the symptoms started. Sudden loss of balance or sudden trouble seeing can also be signs.

Why the time matters

Some of the best treatments — medicines that dissolve the clot or a procedure to pull it out — only work if they are started within the first hours. Do not wait to see whether it gets better, and do not drive the person yourself if an ambulance can come.

Why the opposite side?

The pathways for movement cross over in the lower brainstem, so a stroke in the left side of the brain weakens the right side of the body. A stroke in the left hemisphere often affects speech too, because language areas are usually on the left.

Lowering the risk

High blood pressure is the biggest risk factor. Not smoking, moving every day, eating less salt and checking blood pressure regularly protect the brain for life.

This page is for learning, not medical advice. In an emergency call 103.
