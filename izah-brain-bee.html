What is the Brain Bee?

3 min read · AXON

The world’s neuroscience competition for school students — how it works and how to prepare.

A competition about the brain

The International Brain Bee is a neuroscience competition for high-school students, usually aged 13 to 19. It was started in the late 1990s by the neuroscientist Norbert Myslinski to get young people excited about the brain and to bring more of them into brain science and medicine.

From local to world level

Countries hold local and national competitions. The national champion goes on to the world championship, where students from many countries compete in several rounds.

What the rounds test

The rounds go beyond memorising facts. In the identification round you name structures on a brain, a section or a slide. In the patient-diagnosis round you read a case, choose tests and name the disorder. Written questions cover everything from neurons and the senses to memory, sleep and brain diseases.

How to prepare on AXON

Start with the short lessons, then practise one question at a time with explanations. Use the 3D atlases and the identification drill for the anatomy round, the clinical cases for diagnosis, and the timed mock test to rehearse the written round. A little every day works better than a lot the night before.
