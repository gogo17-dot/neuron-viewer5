What does sleep do for the brain?

3 min read · AXON

A third of your life is spent asleep. Your brain is busy the whole time.

Sleep comes in cycles

A night of sleep is made of cycles of roughly 90 minutes. Each cycle goes from light sleep into deep, slow-wave sleep and then into REM sleep, when the eyes move quickly under the lids and most vivid dreams happen. Deep sleep comes mostly early in the night, REM sleep mostly towards the morning.

What it is for

During sleep the brain replays and strengthens what you learned during the day, which is why sleeping after studying helps you remember it. Sleep also restores attention and mood: after a bad night, the parts of the brain that keep emotions in check work less well.

Who controls it

A small area of the hypothalamus works as the body’s master clock and follows daylight. In the evening, darkness lets the hormone melatonin rise, which tells the body it is night. Bright screens late at night can delay this signal.

How much?

Sleep experts recommend 8–10 hours a night for teenagers aged 13 to 18 and at least 7 hours for adults. A regular bedtime, a dark room and no screens just before sleep make the biggest difference.
