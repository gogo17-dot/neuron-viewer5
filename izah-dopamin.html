Dopamine: more than pleasure

3 min read · AXON

Often called the “happy chemical”, dopamine is really about movement, motivation and learning.

Where it comes from

Dopamine is made by small groups of neurons deep in the midbrain, mainly in the substantia nigra and the ventral tegmental area. From there, their axons spread to large parts of the brain along a few main pathways.

Movement

One pathway runs from the substantia nigra to the striatum in the basal ganglia and helps start and smooth out movement. In Parkinson’s disease these neurons slowly die, and by the time symptoms appear a large share of them is already lost — causing tremor, stiffness and slowness.

Motivation and learning

Another pathway runs to the nucleus accumbens and the frontal cortex. Experiments in the 1990s showed that dopamine neurons fire most when a reward is better than expected, and dip when it is worse — they signal a “prediction error” that helps the brain learn what is worth doing. So dopamine is less about pleasure itself and more about wanting and learning.

Why it matters for habits

Many addictive drugs and attention-grabbing apps act on this reward system, which is why they can be hard to stop. Knowing how it works is the first step to using it well — for example, by rewarding yourself after studying instead of before.
