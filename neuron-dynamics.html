What you are seeing

Between spikes the membrane acts like a leaky capacitor: injected current charges it; leak pulls voltage back toward rest (~−70 mV). When V hits threshold, the model fires a spike and resets — a teaching stand-in for action-potential initiation at the axon initial segment.

Real spikes need fast Na⁺ and delayed K⁺ currents (Hodgkin–Huxley). LIF collapses that into threshold + reset so you can feel rheobase: too little current never spikes; just above threshold you get regular firing. Clinically, AIS channelopathies and some antiepileptics change effective threshold and excitability — the same knobs this demo lets you turn.
